Science & Health

UC San Francisco maps a slow dopamine path that can make bitter flavors appealing

UC San Francisco scientists reported in Nature that a slow dopamine pathway linking the digestive system and the brain helps explain how the brain learns to like flavors, including bitter coffee. In mice, stomach nutrients triggered a prolonged dopamine response in the anterior basolateral amygdala; interrupting that pathway stopped the animals from linking a new flavor with calories, and activating it made them prefer a calorie-free flavor. A similar pattern, stronger on the left side, appeared in a PET study of 13 men and an fMRI study of 38 adults.

Level Up

How to put their point back before you reply

Active listening means intentionally engaging with what someone is trying to communicate, not sitting quietly, nodding, keeping eye contact, or saying "I understand." After they finish, paraphrase their point in your own words and check that you have it right, then ask an open-ended question. The Program on Negotiation at Harvard Law School describes this mix as paraphrasing, inquiry, and acknowledgment, and treats acknowledgment as recognizing the other side's concern without agreeing to it.

Sources: wikipedia.org · harvard.edu

How to practice the weak chunk with immediate feedback

It is not the same as improving a skill to only repeat what you can already perform. Psychologist K. Anders Ericsson described deliberate practice as breaking a skill into parts, focusing on a weaker chunk at a more challenging level, and pairing that work with immediate coaching feedback. He stressed deliberate or purposeful practice in building skill, along with time, resources, and access to expert coaches. If the feedback is not appropriate, extra repetition tends to be ineffective or even detrimental to learning.

Today's field: Law (U.S. legal concepts)

  • Hearsay. Hearsay is an out-of-court statement offered in court to prove that what the speaker said is true. If a witness says Susan told them Tom was in town, that is hearsay if used to prove Tom was in town, because Susan is not available for cross-examination. Courts generally exclude hearsay because the original speaker's credibility cannot be tested. Federal trials follow Article VIII of the Federal Rules of Evidence, Rules 801-807, which include exceptions such as an excited utterance made during a startling event. [cornell.edu · wikipedia.org]
  • Summary judgment. Summary judgment is a judgment entered for one party against another without a full trial. Under Rule 56 of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, the movant must show there is no genuine dispute as to any material fact and that it is entitled to judgment as a matter of law. The court views the evidence in the light most favorable to the non-movant. A judge may grant partial summary judgment, such as on liability while leaving damages for trial, and the procedure does not apply to criminal cases. [cornell.edu · wikipedia.org]
  • Strict scrutiny. Strict scrutiny is the highest standard U.S. courts use to review government action that burdens a fundamental right or involves a suspect classification, including race, religion, national origin, and alienage. The other two standards are intermediate scrutiny and the rational basis test. The action starts from a presumption of unconstitutionality, and the government must show it was narrowly tailored to further a compelling government interest and was the least restrictive means to further that interest. When the government cannot make that showing, the court strikes the law down. [cornell.edu · wikipedia.org]
