Level Up

Show an AI model a few examples of the output you want

When you need an AI model to match a format or tone, such as a client update, paste a few short samples of finished work into the prompt instead of only describing what you want. Anthropic's prompting guide calls examples one of the most reliable ways to steer format, tone and structure, and recommends three to five varied ones that mirror the real task. OpenAI's guide likewise advises pairing diverse inputs with the desired outputs. Label the samples, for instance inside "example" tags, so the model treats them as patterns rather than instructions.

Sources: Anthropic · OpenAI

Write a specific thank-you letter and read it to the person

Pick someone who helped you and whom you never properly thanked, and spend about 10 minutes writing a letter that names exactly what they did and how it affected you. In a 2005 randomized, placebo-controlled study led by psychologist Martin Seligman, writing and delivering such a letter produced the largest happiness boost of five exercises at one month, though the effect faded within months. UC Berkeley's Greater Good Science Center says reading it to the person, in person or by video, helps more than writing alone, and suggests repeating the practice about every six weeks.

Sources: American Psychologist · UC Berkeley Greater Good Science Center

Learn

Today's field: Finance & economics

  • Price elasticity of demand. Price elasticity of demand measures how strongly buyers react to a price change: the percentage change in the quantity bought divided by the percentage change in price. If a 10% discount lifts sales by 20%, demand is elastic (the figure is above 1); if it lifts sales by only 5%, demand is inelastic (below 1). Goods with many close substitutes, such as a single brand of TV, tend to be elastic, while necessities with few substitutes, such as electricity, tend to be inelastic. This matters for sellers because cutting the price raises total revenue when demand is elastic but lowers it when demand is inelastic. [OpenStax Principles of Economics, Rice University · Brigham Young University-Idaho]
  • Real interest rate. The real interest rate is the interest rate after subtracting inflation, so it shows whether your purchasing power is actually growing or shrinking. The rate a bank advertises on a loan or savings account is the nominal rate. For example, a deposit paying 2.5% a year while prices rise 3% has a real return of about minus 0.5%: you end the year with more money, but it buys slightly less. Because future inflation is not known in advance, lenders and economists usually work with expected inflation when a loan or investment is made. The simple subtraction is an approximation that works best when interest rates and inflation are low. [Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis · European Central Bank]
