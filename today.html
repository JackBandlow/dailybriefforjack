How to weigh research on memory for unfinished tasks

The Zeigarnik effect, named after Bluma Zeigarnik, postulates that people remember unfinished or interrupted tasks better than completed ones. She published the report in 1927 in Psychologische Forschung, after Kurt Lewin noticed a waiter recalled unpaid orders better than paid ones. Later replication studies found no significant difference in recall, and the effect's reliability has been a matter of some controversy. A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis found no memory advantage for unfinished tasks and concluded that the Zeigarnik effect lacks universal validity.

Sources: springer.com · wikipedia.org

How to use the pull to resume a task you already started

The Ovsiankina effect is an urge to complete tasks previously initiated, and it is different from the Zeigarnik effect. Maria Ovsiankina, a colleague of Zeigarnik, studied how interruption affects the tendency to resume a task at the next opportunity. A 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis found a general tendency to resume tasks and concluded that the Ovsiankina effect represents a general tendency. In 1928 she published on the resumption of interrupted actions in Psychologische Forschung, volume 11, pages 302-379.

Sources: springer.com · wikipedia.org

Today's field: Construction & engineering

  • Building information modeling. Building information modeling is an approach for generating and managing digital representations of the physical and functional characteristics of buildings or other physical assets. Those files can be extracted, exchanged, or networked to support decisions about a built asset. Many costs of design, construction, and operation come from relying on drawings as the description of record of the building. In 1974, Eastman and others outlined the Building Description System, a computer system for storing and manipulating design information for design, construction, and operational analysis. [ed.gov · nist.gov]
  • Lateral force. Lateral forces are sideways loads on a structure, typically wind and seismic loads. A shear wall is designed to resist in-plane lateral forces, meaning loads parallel to the plane of the wall. Seismic design uses static and dynamic procedures to calculate lateral forces and total base shear. One static method is the equivalent lateral force procedure. [fema.gov · nist.gov]
  • Tuned mass damper. A tuned mass damper, also known as a harmonic absorber or seismic damper, is a device mounted in structures to reduce mechanical vibrations. It is a mass on one or more damped springs, tuned to a frequency similar to the resonant frequency of the object it is mounted to, and it reduces the object's maximum amplitude while weighing much less than that object. These devices can prevent discomfort, damage, or structural failure, and they are used in power transmission, automobiles, and buildings. They are also used in high-rise construction. [purdue.edu · mit.edu]
