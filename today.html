Level Up

Start a review by recalling, not rereading

Retrieval practice means deliberately bringing information to mind instead of rereading or reviewing notes. Psychologists have known for over a century that repeatedly retrieving information, as opposed to re-studying it, strongly enhances long-term retention, and the benefit holds across many materials and testing formats. A simple version is a brain dump: write down everything you can remember from a previous lesson or meeting before you look at your notes. Then check what you missed and review only that.

Sources: retrievalpractice.org · nih.gov

Turn a goal into an if-then plan

An implementation intention is an if-then plan that spells out when, where and how you will act on a goal, linking a specific situation to a specific response. A meta-analysis by Peter Gollwitzer and Paschal Sheeran of 94 independent tests with 8461 participants found these plans had a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment. Pick one goal you keep postponing, name the exact cue, such as a time or place, and write the action you will take when it arrives.

Sources: archive.org · wikipedia.org

Learn

Today's field: Construction & engineering

  • Critical path method. The critical path method is the most widely used way to schedule a construction project. Planners list every activity, how long it takes and which tasks must finish first. The critical path is the longest chain of dependent activities, and its length is the minimum time needed to finish the project. If any activity on the critical path is delayed, the completion of the project will be delayed by an equal amount. [cmu.edu · vt.edu]
  • Rebar. Rebar, short for reinforcing bar, is steel added to concrete to make it stronger. Concrete resists crushing, but its tensile strength is only about 10% of its compressive strength, while steel is strong in tension. Steel and concrete both expand and contract to about the same degree with temperature changes, so they work well together. Most rebar has raised lugs, or deformations, rolled into the surface so the concrete grips the bar. [crsi.org · concrete.org]
  • Retaining wall. A retaining wall is a structure built to provide lateral support for a mass of earth or other material where a grade separation is required. In plain terms, it holds soil in place so the ground can sit at two different levels. Retaining walls stay stable through their own weight, their own weight plus the weight of the soil they hold, or a tieback system. [wa.gov · dot.gov]
