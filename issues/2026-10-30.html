Level Up

Turn goals into "if-then" plans

Saying "I'll do my long run this weekend" or "I'll finish the memo this week" sets a goal but no trigger; an implementation intention adds one: "If it is 6:00 a.m. Saturday, then I put on my shoes and start the 2-hour run," or "If my 1:00 p.m. calendar block starts, then I open the memo draft before email." A meta-analysis of 94 independent tests covering 8,461 participants (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006) found that adding if-then plans to goals improved goal attainment by a medium-to-large effect (d = 0.65). Write one if-then plan today for the task you keep deferring, and a second one for the most likely obstacle ("If a client call runs over, then I move the block to 4:00 p.m., not tomorrow").

Prompting AI on long documents: context first, question last, quotes before answers

When you give an AI model a long document such as a contract, deposition or policy manual, paste the document first and put your instructions and question at the end; Anthropic's prompting guide says queries at the end can improve response quality by up to 30% in its tests, and Google's Gemini guide gives the same advice. Then ask the model to pull exact quotes before it answers, for example: "First, list the verbatim clauses relevant to termination and indemnity in <quotes> tags, with section numbers. Then, using only those quotes, summarize Party B's exit rights. If something isn't in the text, say so." This makes the output easy to check against the source, which matters when the work product goes to a client. Both guides also recommend adding a few short examples of the output you want (few-shot prompting) to improve consistency.
