Level Up

Put the bottom line up front in every email and memo

Start each message with your conclusion or the specific request in the first one or two sentences, then add only the background the reader needs to act. The U.S. military calls this BLUF ("bottom line up front"), and federal plain-language guidelines likewise tell writers to put the most important information first. A useful test: if the reader stops after the subject line and first sentence, they should still know what you need and by when. Use a subject line that names the action, such as "DECISION by Fri: approve vendor shortlist."

Harvard Business Review · PlainLanguage.gov

Quiet nagging to-dos by writing a specific plan, not by finishing them

Unfinished tasks tend to keep pulling at your attention, but in a 2011 set of experiments by Masicampo and Baumeister, making a specific plan for an unfinished goal removed the intrusive thoughts and the drag on performance in an unrelated task. Simply thinking about possible plans did not help; the benefit came from committing to a specific plan. Before ending the day, list each open loop and write one line per item: next action, time, place ("Tue 8:00, desk: draft section 2 of the memo"). Then put those lines in your task list or calendar so your brain can stop rehearsing them.

Today's field: Finance & economics

  • Comparative advantage. A person or country has a comparative advantage in something if they give up less of other things to make it. Even someone who is better at everything gains by focusing on what they are relatively best at and trading for the rest. David Ricardo's classic example used England and Portugal, cloth and wine: Portugal was more efficient at both, yet both countries ended up better off by specializing and trading. This is why a top lawyer who types faster than an assistant may still hire the assistant to type. Econlib · Britannica
  • Moral hazard. Moral hazard is when being protected from a loss makes someone take more risk or less care, because someone else pays if things go badly. The classic case is insurance: people with fire insurance may take fewer fire precautions than before. It also arises between bosses and workers, or regulators and banks, when one side cannot see what the other side is doing. Deductibles and copays exist partly to keep insured people sharing some of the cost. Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond · Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond Economic Quarterly · University of Texas Ethics Unwrapped
  • Bond duration. Duration measures how much a bond's price moves when interest rates change. When rates rise, existing bond prices fall, and a higher duration means a bigger move. As a rule of thumb, a bond with a duration of 10 drops about 10% if rates rise 1 percentage point and gains about 10% if they fall by the same amount. Longer maturities raise duration and higher coupons lower it, and the rule is only approximate for large rate moves. FINRA · MSRB
