Level Up

Run a 10-minute premortem before any big commitment

Before kicking off a project, deal or race plan, tell the team (or yourself): "It's six months from now and this failed badly. Write down every reason why," then spend two minutes each listing causes silently before sharing. Psychologist Gary Klein developed this "premortem" method, building on research by Mitchell, Russo and Pennington (1989) finding that imagining an event has already happened ("prospective hindsight") increased people's ability to identify reasons for future outcomes by about 30%. Close by picking the top two or three failure causes and assigning one concrete mitigation and owner to each, for example: "Client data access delayed -> request credentials this week, owner: me."

Harvard Business Review · Journal of Behavioral Decision Making

Set a caffeine cutoff at least 6 hours before bed

In a small randomized, placebo-controlled home study (Drake et al., 2013), 400 mg of caffeine taken even 6 hours before bedtime cut total sleep by more than 1 hour, as measured by an in-home sleep monitor, while participants' own sleep diaries showed no significant effect at 6 hours. If you aim for bed at 10:00 p.m., make 2:00 p.m. your last coffee or pre-workout, and treat heavy late doses as costing you sleep you won't feel losing. The FDA cites 400 mg a day, about two to three 12-oz cups of coffee, as an amount not generally linked with negative effects for most adults; caffeine in energy drinks and gels varies, so check labels on training fuel. Try a two-week cutoff and compare your sleep tracker or morning energy against the prior two weeks.

Journal of Clinical Sleep Medicine · U.S. FDA
