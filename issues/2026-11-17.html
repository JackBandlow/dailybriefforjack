Update: Commerce issues rule to close AI chip loophole

Update: The Commerce Department on Monday issued an interim final rule to close a loophole allowing Chinese companies to access advanced AI chips remotely through overseas data centers. It requires licenses for advanced chip exports to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, and for remote access to companies headquartered in China, with 90 days from the effective date for existing providers. Commerce said the new rule was "written by and at the direction of the Department of Treasury and the National Security Council." It also came after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent criticized Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick on Friday for not doing enough to restrict chip purchases by Megaspeed, the firm at the center of the Nvidia scandal involving Jensen Huang.

Science & Health

U.S. hyperscale data centers used about 1.8% of electricity, study finds

A study led by the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health and the Harvard Data Science Initiative, published Nov. 16 in Nature Sustainability, estimated that 403 U.S. hyperscale data centers used about 82 terawatt-hours of electricity from May 2024 through April 2025, or about 1.8% of total U.S. electricity consumption. Attributable carbon dioxide emissions were about 25.7 million metric tons, comparable to Denmark's annual carbon footprint, according to the paper. Roughly 54% of attributed generation came from fossil fuels, mainly natural gas (38%) and coal (15%).

How to work in 25-minute timed intervals

Work in timed intervals, typically 25 minutes, then take a short break of typically 5-10 minutes. After four of these intervals, take a longer break of typically 20 to 30 minutes. If you are interrupted during an interval, record and postpone the other activity, or abandon that interval. Francesco Cirillo developed this method, the Pomodoro Technique, in the late 1980s.

Today's field: Law (U.S. legal concepts)

  • Statute of limitations. A statute of limitations is a law passed by a legislative body to set the maximum time after an event within which legal proceedings may be initiated. When that time runs out, a claim may no longer be filed, or if filed it may be dismissed as time-barred. These periods exist in both criminal law and civil law, such as contract law and property law, with details that vary by jurisdiction. A civil action for personal injury or death arising out of a maritime tort must be brought within 3 years after the cause of action arose. In many jurisdictions, there is no time limit for dealing with particularly serious crimes. [wikipedia.org · cornell.edu]
  • Stare decisis. Stare decisis means "to stand by things decided." Under this doctrine, a past judicial decision serves as precedent, an authority for later cases with identical or similar facts or similar legal issues. Courts are required to apply the law in the same manner to cases with the same facts, which promotes consistency and predictability. If the facts or issues of a case differ from those in a previous case, the previous case cannot be precedent. Precedent is a defining feature that sets common law systems apart from civil law systems. [wikipedia.org · cornell.edu]
  • Standing. Standing, also called locus standi, is the requirement that a party seeking a legal remedy show a sufficient connection to, and harm from, the law or action challenged. In the United States, a person may not bring a suit challenging the constitutionality of a law unless they can demonstrate that they are or will imminently be harmed by the law. Otherwise, the court will rule that the plaintiff lacks standing to bring the suit and will dismiss it without considering the merits of the claim of unconstitutionality. [wikipedia.org · cornell.edu]
